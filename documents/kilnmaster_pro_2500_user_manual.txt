SECTION 1: PHYSICAL SPECIFICATIONS & MASONRY STRUCTURAL DATA
================================================================================

1.1 CHAMBER VOLUME & EXTERIOR DIMENSIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Overall External Dimensions (L x W x H):  2800 mm x 1650 mm x 2400 mm
Internal Firing Chamber Volume:          1,250 Liters (44.1 cu. ft.)
Usable Stacking Floor Area:              1.8 Square Meters
Chimney Stack Height (above arch dome):  3,500 mm
Net Dry Weight (Assembled Masonry):     3,120 kg (6,878 lbs)
Combustion Chamber Door Opening:         750 mm x 900 mm (Arched Top)

1.2 REFRACTORY BRICK COMPOSITION & INSULATION RATINGS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Inner Hot-Face Layer:            K-26 High-Alumina Firebrick (2600°F / 1426°C rating)
Outer Insulating Layer:          K-23 Lightweight Insulating Firebrick (IFB)
Sub-Base Insulation:             Calcium Silicate Board (100 mm thickness)
Mortar Specification:            High-Alumina Heat-Set Refractory Mortar
Arch Steel Framework:            Heavy-Gauge Angle Iron with Turnbuckle Tie-Rods

1.3 THERMAL CAPACITANCE & PEAK FIRING TOLERANCES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Maximum Operating Temperature:   1350°C (2462°F / Pyrometric Cone 12)
Recommended Continuous Temp:     1280°C to 1320°C (Cone 10 to Cone 11)
Thermal Shock Limit:             Cooling rate must not exceed 100°C per hour above 600°C
Heat Retention Period:           24 to 36 hours post-stoking cutoff

1.4 FLUE STACK & CHIMNEY DRAFT SPECIFICATIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Flue Cross-Sectional Area:       300 mm x 300 mm (Square Profile)
Draft Mechanism:                 Natural Passive Thermal Convection (Up-Draft System)
Damper Construction:             Heavy Silicon Carbide Plate (25 mm thickness)
Maximum Air Throughput Velocity:  4.2 meters per second at 1000°C chamber temp

SECTION 2: SAFETY WARNINGS, THERMAL HAZARDS & REGULATORY COMPLIANCE
================================================================================

2.1 SAFETY ALERT CONVENTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[DANGER]:  Indicates an imminently hazardous condition involving extreme temperatures 
           or structural collapse which WILL result in severe burns or death.
[WARNING]: Indicates a situation which COULD result in serious injury, carbon 
           monoxide poisoning, or structural fire.
[CAUTION]: Indicates a condition that may cause physical burns, equipment damage, 
           or ruined ceramic wares.
[NOTICE]:  Critical advice regarding wood fuel selection, draft adjustment, and 
           refractory longevity.

2.2 EXTREME HEAT & BURN PREVENTION PROTOCOLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[DANGER] EXTREME SURFACE TEMPERATURE HAZARD
During peak firing, external brick surfaces exceed 120°C (248°F) and interior 
chambers reach up to 1350°C (2462°F). Direct contact will cause catastrophic burns.

1. Operators MUST wear heat-resistant leather stoking aprons, long-cuffed heavy 
   Kevlar/Nomex insulated gloves rated for 1000°C minimum, and full-face thermal shields.
2. NEVER look directly into open spyholes or stoke holes without wearing shade 3 
   or shade 5 infrared-blocking safety goggles to prevent retinal burn.
3. Establish a 2.5-meter safety perimeter around the kiln structure during firing operations.
4. Keep all unauthorized personnel, children, and pets clear of the firing site.

2.3 TOXIC FUME VENTILATION & AIRWAY PROTECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[WARNING] COMBUSTION GAS & GLAZE OFF-GASSING HAZARD
Burning wood and vitrifying ceramic glazes release toxic gases, including Carbon 
Monoxide (CO), Sulfur Dioxide (SO2), Fluorine gas, and heavy metal vapors (lead, cobalt).

1. The KilnMaster Pro-2500 MUST be installed in an open-air shed or a dedicated 
   fireproof facility equipped with high-volume canopy exhaust hoods.
2. Operators MUST wear dual-filter respirators equipped with organic vapor and P100 
   particulate cartridges during reduction firing and fuel stoking.
3. Install dual industrial Carbon Monoxide alarms inside the firing building at a 
   distance of 3 meters from the kiln base.

2.4 FIRE SUPPRESSION & COMBUSTIBLE CLEARANCE ZONES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Maintain a minimum of 2.0 meters clearance between the kiln walls and any 
  combustible timber walls, wooden roofs, or stored firewood stacks.
- Maintain a minimum of 4.0 meters vertical clearance above the top of the chimney stack.
- Keep two 10kg Class A/B/C dry chemical fire extinguishers and a 50-liter sand 
  bucket within 5 meters of the stoking pit at all times.

SECTION 3: UNPACKING, SITE PREPARATION & INITIAL MASONRY ASSEMBLY
================================================================================

3.1 FOUNDATION PAD & STRUCTURAL WEIGHT BEARING REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Sub-floor Spec: Reinforced poured concrete pad, minimum 250 mm thickness.
- Load Capacity: Must support at least 5,000 kg (11,000 lbs) point load without settling.
- Drainage: Sloped slightly (1% gradient away from the kiln mouth) to prevent water 
  pooling under the base bricks.

3.2 MORTAR CURING & BRICK STACKING PROCEDURES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Lay down the calcium silicate sub-base insulation boards on the level concrete pad.
2. Mix high-alumina refractory mortar with clean water to a smooth butter-like consistency.
3. Lay the first course of high-density firebricks for the hearth floor, using thin 
   1.5 mm mortar joints.
4. Build sidewalls upward in alternating running-bond courses.
5. Set the wooden arch formwork into position and lay the key-arch roof firebricks.
6. Drive the center key-bricks into place using a wooden mallet; tighten metal frame tie-rods.

3.3 DAMPER GATE INSTALLATION & CHIMNEY COUPLING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Mount the cast-iron damper frame guide channels onto the rear flue assembly.
2. Slide the silicon carbide damper plate into the guide channel slot, ensuring 
   smooth manual sliding movement.
3. Stack the chimney brick flue upward to the designated height, anchoring the 
   stack to external structural supports every 1.5 meters using steel straps.

3.4 INITIAL PRE-HEAT CURING SCHEDULE (WATER-SMOKING PHASE)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[NOTICE] DO NOT FIRE THE KILN TO HIGH TEMPERATURES IMMEDIATELY AFTER ASSEMBLY.
Newly laid refractory mortar contains residual moisture that must be slowly evaporated.

Phase 1 (Day 1): Light a small kindling fire in the firebox; keep chamber temperature 
                 below 100°C (212°F) for 12 hours with damper 100% open.
Phase 2 (Day 2): Increase heat to 250°C (482°F) over 8 hours; maintain for 6 hours.
Phase 3 (Day 3): Ramp heat slowly to 600°C (1112°F) over 10 hours, then allow to cool naturally.

SECTION 4: KILN ARCHITECTURE & MECHANICAL CONTROLS
================================================================================

4.1 MAIN COMBUSTION CHAMBER & FIREBOX GRATE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The lower firebox features cast-iron heavy-duty grate bars that hold burning timber off 
the ash floor. Primary combustion air enters from under the grate bars, drawing oxygen upward 
through the fuel bed into the main chamber.

4.2 HIGH-ALUMINA ARCH CEILING & HEAT RETENTION DOME
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
The sprung arch ceiling is calculated at a 60-degree radius to reflect radiant heat down 
onto the ceramic ware shelves while directing combustion flame paths toward the rear 
down-draft bagwall before entering the exit flue.

4.3 MANUAL SLIDING FLUE DAMPER & AIR INTAKE VENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Primary Air Dampers: Cast-iron hinged doors located at the front of the firebox pit.
- Secondary Air Ports: Adjustable sliding brick covers located above the main firedoor.
- Flue Damper Plate: Silicon carbide slab at the chimney base used to regulate stack draft.

4.4 SPYHOLES & PYROMETRIC CONE VIEWING PORTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Three tapered ceramic spyhole ports are built into the front door wall (Top, Middle, Bottom). 
These allow the artisan to visually inspect pyrometric cone bending and glaze melting behavior 
inside the chamber during firing.

SECTION 5: FIRING MANUAL & THERMAL CONTROL PROCEDURES
================================================================================

5.1 FIREWOOD FUEL SELECTION & MOISTURE REQUIREMENTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Wood Type: Hardwoods (Oak, Maple, Hickory) for sustained heat; Softwoods (Pine, Spruce) 
  for long-flame glaze effects and rapid temperature spikes.
- Moisture Content: Moisture levels MUST be between 12% and 18% (measured via pin meter).
- Dimensions: Split logs, 500 mm to 700 mm length, 50 mm to 100 mm thickness.
- DO NOT USE: Pressure-treated wood, painted timber, plywood, or salt-driftwood.

5.2 LOW-TEMPERATURE PRE-HEATING & BISQUE FIRING STEPS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. Load greenware pots on silicon carbide shelves using 3-point stilt posts.
2. Brick up the main chamber doorway leaving a 50 mm gap at the top for moisture exhaust.
3. Build a small wood fire in the firebox pit using thin softwood kindling.
4. Maintain a slow temperature rise rate of 50°C per hour up to 500°C.
5. Complete doorway brick sealing; seal gaps with a clay/sand mixture (wadding).
6. Continue firing to 980°C (Cone 06) for bisque vitrification.

5.3 HIGH-TEMPERATURE GLAZE FIRING WORKFLOWS (CONE 10 / 1300°C)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. After bisque cooling, load glazed ceramic wares with appropriate shelf-wash coating.
2. Position pyrometric cone packs (Guide, Target, Guard cones) aligned with spyholes.
3. Fire aggressively with dry hardwood, stoking every 10 to 15 minutes.
4. Monitor rate of rise: Aim for 100°C per hour between 600°C and 1100°C.
5. Reduce stoking intervals to every 5 minutes when approaching 1250°C.

5.4 REDUCTION ATMOSPHERIC CONTROL VIA FLUE ADJUSTMENT
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
To achieve rich copper-red and celadon glaze effects:
1. Upon reaching 1000°C (Cone 011), push the main chimney damper in by 60%.
2. Close primary air intake dampers to restrict oxygen supply to the firebox.
3. Heavy flame will stream out of the upper spyhole (positive chamber pressure).
4. Maintain smoky reduction atmosphere for 2 to 3 hours or until Target Cone bends.

5.5 NATURAL COOLING CYCLE & UNLOADING PROTOCOLS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1. When Target Cone 10 completely bends flat, execute final heavy stoke and close 
   all damper ports, air vents, and stoke holes completely (passive soak).
2. Allow kiln to cool sealed for a MINIMUM of 24 to 36 hours.
3. DO NOT unbrick the main door until interior chamber temperature drops below 150°C.
4. Opening the doorway above 200°C will cause severe thermal shock dunting (cracking) 
   of ceramic pieces and damage to firebricks.

SECTION 6: PREVENTIVE MAINTENANCE SCHEDULE & REPAIR GUIDELINES
================================================================================

6.1 PRE-FIRING BRICK & MORTAR INSPECTION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Inspect firebox arch for loose, shifting, or sagging bricks.
[ ] Check floor bricks for glaze drips; scrape clean and re-apply alumina shelf wash.
[ ] Verify chimney damper plate slides freely without binding inside channel.
[ ] Clear ash build-up from under grate bars to ensure unimpeded air intake.

6.2 POST-FIRING ASH EXTRACTION & GRATE CLEANING
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Wait 48 hours post-firing until ash pit is completely cold.
[ ] Shovel out wood ash using a non-sparking aluminum shovel into a steel ash drum.
[ ] Inspect cast-iron grate bars for severe warping, oxidation, or thermal sag.
[ ] Spray down ash pit floor with fine water mist to settle toxic fly-ash dust.

6.3 REFRACTORY PATCHING & CRACKING REPAIR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
- Minor hairline surface cracks (< 3 mm wide) are normal due to thermal expansion.
- For wider cracks (> 5 mm): Mix 50% kaolin clay, 50% grog with water glass (sodium silicate).
- Dampen target brick surface with water; press mixture firmly into crack using a putty knife.
- Allow patch to dry for 24 hours prior to next thermal cycle.

6.4 CHIMNEY SOOT SWEEPING & FLUE CLEARANCE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
[ ] Perform chimney sweep every 10 firings or every 6 months.
[ ] Lower a wire flue brush down the chimney stack to dislodge creosote and fly-ash.
[ ] Vacuum out dislodged debris from the rear collector box under the flue damper.

SECTION 7: TROUBLESHOOTING GUIDE & FIRING DEFECT ANALYSIS
================================================================================

7.1 TEMPERATURE STAGNATION & DRAFT STARVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Symptom: Kiln temperature stalls between 1100°C and 1150°C and will not rise.
  Possible Cause A: Wood fuel moisture content is too high (> 20%).
    -> Solution: Switch immediately to split, kiln-dried softwood boards.
  Possible Cause B: Ash pit is choked, blocking primary combustion air.
    -> Solution: Carefully rake out accumulation under grate using long iron poker.
  Possible Cause C: Flue damper is closed too far, choking natural draft.
    -> Solution: Pull damper outward in 10 mm increments until draft roar resumes.

7.2 GLAZE BLISTERING, PIN-HOLING, AND THERMAL SHOCK FAULTS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Symptom: Glaze surfaces contain tiny bubbles, craters, or pin-holes.
  -> Cause: Firing proceeded too quickly through the glaze-melting phase (1100°C - 1200°C).
  -> Solution: Implement a 30-minute temperature hold (soak) at peak heat before shutdown.

Symptom: Wares crack with sharp, clean ringing fracture lines (Dunting).
  -> Cause: Kiln was opened while chamber temperature was above 200°C.
  -> Solution: Allow longer natural cooling cycle; never force-cool with fans.

7.3 STRUCTURAL BRICK SPALLING & CRACK DIAGNOSTICS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Symptom: Outer faces of firebricks flake off in large curved chips (Spalling).
  -> Cause: Moisture trapped inside dense firebrick rapidly turned to steam during heating.
  -> Solution: Extend low-temperature water-smoking phase on all subsequent firings.
